## D-03 Standalone timelapse without a worker or paid source (agreed in principle; prototype works)
**Question asked by the owner:** can the timelapse run without a Cloudflare Worker and without paid sources?

**How tonguesfromspace does it** (read in `worker/worker.js`, `js/03-imagery.js`): the page calls a Worker that holds Copernicus Data Space OAuth client id/secret (up to 15 accounts), forwards Sentinel Hub Statistical API requests (scene scoring by SCL cloud/snow) and Process API requests (rendering), and caches in KV. Needs secrets, quota and a paid Cloudflare plan. **Not reusable for GfS.**

**Alternative checked 2026-11-03:** Element 84 *Earth Search* (public STAC, Sentinel-2 L2A as Cloud Optimized GeoTIFFs on the public AWS bucket `sentinel-cogs`).
- `POST https://earth-search.aws.element84.com/v1/search` answers 200 with `access-control-allow-origin: *` (curl, Origin header set).
- A COG asset (`.../TCI.tif`) answers byte-range requests (206) with `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, `Accept-Ranges: bytes`.
- Result: a **static page in the browser** can search scenes and read only the needed pixels of a polygon (e.g. with geotiff.js), no key, no server. This could be one self-contained HTML file hosted on GitHub Pages, opened from the Results page with the polygon passed in the URL.

**Prototype result (2026-11-03):** `index.html` works in headless Chromium: Borneo (5.85 N) and Alps (46.5 N) polygons produce real frames, with scene choice and composites as in D-04. Pages, scenes and the Earth Search STAC were read live; nothing is cached or stored.

**Not yet verified:** terms of use / licence of Earth Search and the bucket (to read, Q-03); the Streamlit side (how to pass a polygon from the Results map to a link, Q-04); performance of reading many scenes in-browser; whether Sentinel-2 imagery is enough for small polygons (population polygons in the example are about 5 to 20 km across, fine at 10 m).

**Alternatives:** (a) Copernicus Browser deep link per polygon (no code, but no timelapse of our own, user must use its UI); (b) Sentinel Hub EO Browser link (same); (c) Google Earth Engine timelapse (needs an account, not "no key"); (d) own worker with CDSE credentials (needs secrets and quota: rejected by the constraints).

Stop-gap link to the Copernicus Browser: rejected by the owner (D-02).

## D-04 Scene choice per year (implemented in `index.html`, 2026-11-03; to be reviewed)
1. **Season** (the "relevant summer"): northern hemisphere June to September; southern December to March (season named after the year it ends in); **tropics (|latitude| < 23.5): there is no summer**, so the three months with the lowest mean tile cloud cover (`eo:cloud_cover` of all catalogue scenes over the polygon, all years) are used, the same months every year so frames are comparable. User can override the months on the page or with `from`/`to` in the link. *Assumption to confirm with the leader: Q-06. The tile cloud cover is a heuristic, not a climate record.*
2. **Single scene first:** scenes of the same day (several MGRS tiles) are merged; up to 10 days per year, lowest tile cloud first, are scored by the **SCL** band inside the polygon only; bad = no data, defective/saturated (1), cloud shadow (3), cloud (8, 9), thin cirrus (10); snow/ice (11) counts as clear. The scene with the least bad pixels is taken if **at most 2 %** are bad. (First try 5 %: visible clouds remained in the Borneo test, so 2 %.)
3. **Fallback composite:** otherwise the **per-pixel median** of the clear pixels of up to 12 best days in the same season window; pixels with no clear value stay dark grey and the share is reported. This is the "quarterly mosaic" of the owner's rule; for N/S hemisphere the window is the 4-month summer, not exactly a calendar quarter. *Question whether that is acceptable: Q-07.*
4. Pictures are the delivered **TCI** (true colour) product, cut to the polygon area, never recoloured. Resolution: 10 m pixel at best, at most 800 px per side (coarser overviews are read for large polygons).
5. First year **2017**: Earth Search has no L2A over Borneo in 2016 (search returned 0 scenes on 2026-11-03); 2017 is sparse there (37 scenes in the whole year).
6. The year range stops at the previous year unless the current season has ended.

Known limits of the prototype: cloud shadow outside SCL's detection can remain; a polygon spanning two UTM zones is not handled (each scene is read in its own CRS, it works, but not tested); heavy polygons (> 100 km across) are read from coarse overviews and are slow; no download/export yet; tested only in headless Chromium, not Safari/Firefox/phone.

## D-06 Fixes found while testing the wire (2026-11-03)
- **Search robustness:** the first wire test failed with "Failed to fetch" (CORS error in the console) on the tropics catalogue search. Earth Search answers some requests in a burst with an error that carries no CORS header; `stacPost` now retries up to 4 times with a growing pause. The cause was not established beyond that (curl bursts of 12 requests all returned 200), so it is a workaround, not a diagnosis.
- **Paging:** a year can have more than 200 scenes over a polygon (2018 over Sabah: 243); the page follows the API's `next` links (max 10 pages), de-duplicates by scene id and stops when a page brings nothing new. The API's page tokens were seen to step backwards in date between pages; results were still complete by id.
- **Tropics catalogue search** asks only for id, date and cloud cover (`fields`), to keep the answers small.
- **Season no longer depends on the years shown:** the tropical clearest three months were first computed from the years on screen, so the same polygon gave Mar to May for 2019 to 2021 and Jul to Sep for 2022 to 2023. It now always uses every full year since 2017 (D-04.1 amended). Also seen: for the mock `pop_3`, 2022 and 2023 had no single clear scene and used composites of 10 and 9 days (0.1 % and 0.0 % of the polygon left without a clear pixel).

- **Faster scoring** (measured on the mock `pop_3`, Mar to May 2022 to 2024, three fresh browser contexts each, `from`/`to` given so the season search is excluded): old 34 s and 49 s, new 22 s and 24 s for three years. - **Loading display:** a progress bar and headline "Loading: n of N years finished, k in progress, m from saved pictures", one chip per year (waiting, searching, scoring x/y, composite, ready, saved, none), chips of finished years can be clicked, the picture area appears with the first finished year, and the final line says how many seconds it took.

Not shared between users or devices; lost if the user clears site data or uses a private window (the page then simply works without). Time to live 30 days (1 day for "no picture" results), at most 120 years kept, oldest dropped first. `CACHE_VERSION` in `index.html` must be raised whenever scoring or rendering changes. Link "forget saved pictures" in the footer. Tested: second open of the same population took 0 s (all chips "saved"). A different `from`/`to` or polygon is a new key.
- **Test polygon in the mockup:** `test_alps` (Aletsch region) is in the mockup's drop-down, labelled as a test, so the northern-summer path can be tried there. It is not a population and is not in the table.

## D-09 Delete a year, show the best single scene (owner request, 2026-11-03)
The request ("from the scene list, make it possible to delete individual scenes or show the best non-composite image") was read as:

## D-13 High-resolution export (owner, 2026-11-03: "make sure export happens with proper high resolution")
Before, every export was the 800 px picture shown on screen (about 20 to 55 m per pixel for a 15 to 45 km polygon), so a PNG or video was coarser than Sentinel-2's own 10 m.
- **New "Quality" menu** in the export panel: **High (10 m pixels)**, the default, and **As shown** (the old behaviour). The panel states the sizes that will come out, e.g. "PNG 1927 × 1924 px (10 m per pixel), video 1920 × 1917 px (10 m per pixel), GIF 1000 × 999 px (19 m per pixel)".
- **How High works:** the exact same scenes (ids kept in the frame, fetched again from Earth Search) are read again on a finer grid, from the full-resolution COG (10 m), and composites are recomputed on that grid with the same median rule. Nothing is sharpened or invented; the pixels are the delivered TCI pixels. Re-read pictures are kept in memory for the session so repeated exports are quick.
- **Size caps (long side):** PNG 2500 px (the same limit the TSF project works to), video 1920 px, GIF 1000 px (GIF files and encoding explode beyond that). A polygon too wide for 10 m inside the cap gets the finest pixel that fits, and the panel says so. These caps are my choice, not tested on phones; a very large composite can run out of memory in the browser, in which case the export says "Export failed" and "As shown" still works.
- **Video:** a picture smaller than 1080 px on the long side is enlarged in **whole pixels without smoothing** so that the video is not tiny (only in High); bitrate scales with size (8 to 25 Mbit/s).
- **Measured** on the Borneo box 16 km wide (Mar to May, 2024 and 2025, composites): screen picture 800 px = 24 m per pixel; High PNG 1927 × 1924 px, 10 m, took 18 s (including re-reading the scenes); "As shown" PNG 800 px, instant; High MP4 (1920 × 2056 px, 515 kB, 2 frames) took 33 s. A side-by-side crop showed visible new detail in the high version (roofs, shore lines, mud flats) that the 800 px picture smears.
- **Size info set out as a table (owner, 2026-11-06):** the one-line size text is now a heading ("Export size · True colour and NIR side by side"), one row per format (size in px, metres per pixel) and, only when a format misses 10 m, a note in the warning colour naming those formats and the caps. Checked in headless Chromium on a 60 km box at 1280 and 390 px, no page errors.
- **Not tested:** GIF at high quality (only through the size line), Safari/Firefox, phones, large polygons at the caps, playback of the MP4 in other players.

## D-15 NIR view, side by side, zoom and measuring (owner request, 2026-11-06)
- **NIR view:** false colour with near infrared (B08) in red, the TCI red in green and the TCI green in blue, so healthy vegetation shows bright red. Only one extra band is read per scene (B08, 10 m); red and green are taken from the TCI already read. Reflectance = DN × scale + offset from the item's `raster:bands` (checked 2026-11-06 on S2A_50NPM_20231228_0_L2A and S2A_50NPM_20191229_1_L2A: scale 0.0001, offset -0.1, `earthsearch:boa_offset_applied: true`), stretched 0 to 0.5 → 0 to 255. Composites take the per-pixel median of NIR like the other bands. Scenes without an https `nir` asset are skipped. `CACHE_VERSION` is `v3` (older saved years have no NIR).
  Alternatives: read red and green as 16-bit bands too (consistent stretch, three times more NIR-view downloads); NIR alone in grey (less readable). Not chosen for cost.
- **View switch** RGB / Both / NIR (remembered per browser). Both: side by side, stacked when the window is narrower than about 530 px. Exports follow the switch; side by side halves the video and GIF size cap per panel.
- **Zoom and pan** shared by both viewers: scroll, double-click, +/−, Fit; drag to pan; up to 24×; pixels shown unsmoothed when zoomed in.
- **Measuring:** Line (great-circle length), Area (spherical polygon area, the OpenLayers formula, plus perimeter in the readout), Erase (click a measurement), "remove all". Enter finishes, Esc cancels, Backspace removes the last point. Measurements are shown on both viewers, not in exports, and are kept until the polygon changes.
- Controls sit in one row under the viewers with Play/Speed; the time slider is shorter, the loading bar thinner (3 px).
- **Tested** 2026-11-06 in headless Chromium on the Borneo box (2021 to 2023): three composites with NIR, zoom 1.8×, a 6.05 km line and a 4.42 km² area, NIR-only view, phone width (stacked), PNG export side by side (5017 × 2496 px at High), no page errors. **Not checked:** measured values against a GIS, Safari/Firefox/touch devices, video and GIF export with both views. Erase by click checked separately: a click away from the line keeps it, a click on it removes it.

## D-16 Keep loading when the tab is in the background (owner request, 2026-11-06)
- **Cause:** geotiff.js 2.1.3 (`BlockedSource.fetch`) awaits `setTimeout(undefined)` before every block request, to batch requests (read in the `dist-browser/geotiff.js` bundle, 2026-11-06). Browsers stretch timers in hidden tabs (Chrome: at least 1 s; after about 5 min hidden, once a minute), so loading crawled when the tab was not in front. Fetches themselves are not throttled.
- **Choice:** a small script before the libraries replaces `window.setTimeout` for zero-delay calls only: those run through a `MessageChannel`, which hidden tabs do not throttle. Calls with a real delay (STAC retry back-off, video recording, URL revoke) go to the browser's own timer unchanged. `clearTimeout` handles both.
- **Alternatives:** run the reading in a Web Worker (worker timers are not throttled, but the whole pipeline would have to move; a bigger change); geotiff without `BlockedSource` (`blockSize: null`, many more small range requests); a silent audio loop to keep the tab "active" (hack, not reliable across browsers).
- **Tested** 2026-11-06 in Chrome for Testing (Chromium 1248, not Playwright, which turns background throttling off), page in a background tab (`visibilityState: hidden`): 20 zero-delay timeouts took 13.6 s with the native timer and 0 ms with the patch. Full run, Alps box `10.0,46.4,10.06,46.44`, 2021 to 2023, hidden the whole time: 9 s with the patch (empty caches) vs 33 s without (browser HTTP cache already warm). **Not checked:** Firefox and Safari (their background throttling differs; the MessageChannel route should help there too), runs longer than 5 min hidden, and a tab that the browser freezes or discards to save memory (Chrome Memory Saver, mobile browsers): no page script can prevent that.

## D-17 Loading message and background map in the viewers (owner request, 2026-11-06)

  - **GfS tool** always opens https://www.gfstool.com/.
- **Closing instead of navigating:** the Results page opens the timelapse in a new tab. A tab that has no history of its own (`history.length` is 1) is closed when Pick a population is clicked, so the user is back on the Results page as they left it (map, chosen population), not on a second, reloaded copy. If the browser refuses to close the tab, the page follows the link after 150 ms. Clicks with Ctrl, Cmd or Shift, and tabs that have their own history, follow the link as normal.
- **Alternatives:** always following the link (leaves two Results tabs open, and Streamlit would rerun from the start); `history.back()` (does nothing in a new tab); `window.opener.focus()` (the mockup opens the page with `rel="noopener"`, and the real tool should as well).
- **Credits:** the footer is now a list of labels with text (Imagery, Pictures, Background map, Measurements, Saved pictures, About); it becomes one column under 600 px. The imagery line links to the AWS Open Data registry entry and to Earth Search. The EOxCloudless credit (D-17) is also listed there, and it still shows in the viewers while the map is visible, as the licence asks.
- **Tested (headless Chromium, 2026-11-06):** from the mockup, picking `test_alps` and clicking Open gave a new tab with Back pointing to the mockup (`history.length` 1). Clicking Pick a population closed that tab. Opened without `back=` and without a referrer, it pointed to `mockup/results.html`, and GfS tool to gfstool.com. `back=javascript:...` was rejected. Both buttons fit in one row at 390 px. The footer was checked at 1280 px and 390 px, with no horizontal scroll at 390 px; all five external footer links returned HTTP 200. **Not checked:** Firefox and Safari. Firefox may refuse to close a tab that the user opened, and then the link is followed instead. Also not checked: the real Streamlit page, which must add `back=` itself (Q-09).

## D-21 Free drawing on the Results page, at most 60 × 60 km (owner request, 2026-11-06)
- **What:** the mockup Results map has draw tools (top left): polygon, rectangle, edit corners, move, remove. One drawn area at a time; drawing again replaces it. It joins the drop-down as "Drawn area (yours)", gets a popup with its size and an Open timelapse button, and opens `index.html` through the same `timelapseUrl` as a population (name "Drawn area"). The population polygons and markers are locked (`pmIgnore`): the edit tools do not touch them. Self-crossing polygons are refused by the draw tool.
- **Library:** Leaflet-Geoman free 2.20.1 (MIT, `npm` registry read 2026-11-06; released 2026-09, newest is 2.20.6), CSS and JS from jsDelivr, no key. Alternative: Leaflet.draw 1.0.4 (last release 2018, known bugs with Leaflet 1.8+).
- **Limit: 60 × 60 km**, i.e. east-west and north-south extent each at most 60 km (owner, 2026-11-06: "max 60x60km"). Measured on the bounding box, because `index.html` builds its picture grid on the bounding box: a long thin polygon costs as much as its box, so an area limit alone would not cap the cost. Extent uses the same formula as `setupGrid()` in `index.html` (without its 8 % margin), area the same spherical formula as the measuring tool.
- **Why 60 km, compared with the populations in the list** (mockup polygons, invented but sized like the screenshot example; areas from the same formula):

  | Population | Longest side | East-west × north-south | Area |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | test_alps | 24 km | 16 × 24 km | 247 km² |
  | pop_1 | 29 km | 29 × 27 km | 517 km² |
  | pop_2 | 34 km | 34 × 32 km | 704 km² |
  | pop_3 | 38 km | 38 × 38 km | 915 km² |
  | pop_4 | 48 km | 38 × 48 km | 1148 km² |

  60 km is about 1.25 times the largest population; a 60 × 60 km square is 3600 km², about 3 times pop_4. The screen picture is capped at 800 px, so at 60 km (70 km with the margin) one pixel is about 87 m, against 10 m at 8 km and 55 to 70 m for pop_3 and pop_4; much larger and the "closer look" shows little. A Sentinel-2 tile is about 110 km wide, so above roughly 60 km an area more often spans two or more tiles per day, and reads multiply. The High export is 10 m only up to about 21 km (PNG cap 2500 px), so all populations here already export coarser than 10 m.
- **Messages:** a live readout in the lower left of the map while drawing or dragging corners ("44.2 × 31.4 km (east-west × north-south), 1037 km²"); over the limit it turns red and says by how much: "Too large: the maximum is 60 × 60 km. East-west is 168.4 km, 108.4 km over; north-south is 75.3 km, 15.3 km over." The same text sits under the map and in the popup, the shape turns red, and the Open button is disabled with that text as its tooltip. The line under the map gives the populations' range for comparison (computed from the list, 29 to 47 km on the longest side).
- **Only on the drawing tool:** the timelapse page itself does not refuse large polygons, so a real GfS population larger than 60 km still opens (the tool's automatic grouping with Distance 100 km can make large polygons).
- **Tested (headless Chromium, 2026-11-06):** polygon 44 × 31 km opens (Open enabled, link 307 characters, timelapse page loads); rectangle 168 × 75 km blocked with the message above; dragging a corner updates the readout live and after release (96 × 46 km still blocked); a smaller rectangle 42 × 29 km replaced it and was enabled; remove takes it out of the drop-down and keeps the populations; in edit mode only the drawn shape's 4 corners get handles; no page errors. **Not checked:** touch devices (no live readout without a mouse; the size shows after the shape is finished), Firefox and Safari, polygons over the antimeridian. The page already scrolls sideways at 390 px (the table, 594 px wide); this was so before the change.
- **Load measured** (headless Chromium, empty cache per run, Turkey, 2021 to 2023, June to September, all three years single clear scenes): 40 × 40 km box `36.03,36.82,36.48,37.18`: 34.7 s, 276 range requests, 105 MB, 58 m per pixel on screen; 60 × 60 km box `35.90,36.73,36.575,37.27`: 21.8 s, 200 requests, 58 MB, 87 m per pixel. So up to 60 km the load does not grow with size (the page reads coarser overviews for larger boxes; the difference between the two runs is mostly which days and tiles were picked, one run each). **The limit is therefore about detail, not speed:** above 60 km a pixel is coarser than about 90 m. Not measured: boxes above 60 km, composite years at 60 km (those read about 8 days per year, D-14).
